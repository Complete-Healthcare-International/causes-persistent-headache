Everyone gets a headache from time to time. A late night, a skipped meal or a stressful deadline can easily trigger one. But when the pain keeps returning or refuses to ease, it deserves attention. Understanding the causes of persistent headache is the first step towards relief, and it helps you decide when a doctor's visit is needed.
This article looks at common triggers, explains how doctors approach the problem, and describes warning signs that should not be ignored. It also outlines how Complete Healthcare International (CHI) can help you find answers.

Why Headaches Linger

Headaches fall into two broad groups. Primary headaches, such as tension type headaches and migraines, are conditions in their own right. Secondary headaches happen because of something else, such as an infection, an eye problem or a medication effect.
When a persistent headache keeps coming back, lifestyle factors are often involved. Poor sleep, dehydration, long hours at a screen and constant stress all play a role. Yet sometimes the cause is less obvious, which is why a proper assessment is useful. A doctor listens to how the pain feels, where it sits, how long it lasts and what seems to set it off. Those details often point towards the right explanation.
 
Common Triggers to Consider
These are some of the everyday factors linked with ongoing headaches.

• Tension in the neck, shoulders and jaw from desk work
 • Irregular or too little sleep
 • Skipped meals or low blood sugar
 • Not drinking enough water
 • Too much caffeine, or sudden cutting back
 • Overuse of pain relievers, which can cause rebound headaches
 • Eye strain or an outdated glasses prescription
 • Hormonal changes
 • Strong smells, bright lights or loud noise
 • Sinus congestion or allergies
 
Keeping a headache diary for two weeks can reveal patterns. Note the time, duration, food, sleep and stress levels.

Warning Signs and Next Steps

Most headaches are not dangerous, but some features need prompt medical attention.

• A sudden, severe headache that feels like the worst of your life
 • Headache with fever, stiff neck or confusion
 • Weakness, numbness, vision changes or difficulty speaking
 • Headache after a head injury
 • Pain that wakes you from sleep or steadily worsens
 • A new pattern of headaches after the age of fifty
 
If any of these appear, seek urgent care. For less urgent situations, book a consultation.
